[A4] — Found via the audit hash-chain test, but a systemic issue, not an audit-only one: SQLite's driver silently returns a NAIVE datetime from a `DateTime(timezone=True)` column on read, even though a tz-aware value was written. `/audit/verify` was failing on an UN-tampered chain because the hash was computed once at append time (tz-aware `at`) and again at verify time on a freshly re-read row (naive `at`, no "+00:00" in the isoformat string) — two different canonical strings, two different hashes, no tampering involved. Fixed at the root with a `UTCDateTime` TypeDecorator in shared/infrastructure/db.py (re-attaches UTC tzinfo on read, exported via the shared facade) and migrated every existing DateTime column (intake's five tables, audit's one) to it, not just audit's. [A4] — A second, related clock bug, inverse of A3's SIM_CLOCK_EPOCH one: `Login` originally computed the session cookie's expiry from the injected (simulated) `Clock`, which starts at SIM_CLOCK_EPOCH (1970) and had not been advanced in a fresh test app — so every login cookie was already "expired" in 1970 and httpx's cookie jar silently dropped it (login returned 200, but no session cookie ever reached the client; every subsequent `/auth/me` call then got 401 for what looked like no reason).
